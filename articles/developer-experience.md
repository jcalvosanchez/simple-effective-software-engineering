## What is Developer Experience?

**Developer experience** (DevEx) refers to the overall experience and satisfaction that developers have when they perform tasks needed to implement a change.

Every element of a developer’s ecosystem (from the systems, tools, documentation and processes that they use to the culture of their organization), influences the effectiveness and quality of software development, contributing to developer productivity, satisfaction and operational [impact](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/my-agile-manifesto-v11-jer%C3%B3nimo-calvo-s%C3%A1nchez-5wlmf/?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block).

Importantly, collaboration acts as a multiplier for DevEx. Developers need to be able to easily communicate and share with each other to do their best work.

## Why does Developer Experience matter?

Developer experience plays a major role on the developer's and team's day-to-day.

- **Increases Productivity and Velocity**. By reducing the time and effort spent on **non-value-add activities** such as dev/local environment setup, waiting on remote environments to test, and rework (fixing defects).
- **Improves Quality and Security**. When it's **easy to debug and test**, developers will do more of it. This will translate to fewer defects being introduced and a more robust and secure solution.
- **Enables experimentation**. A robust and **reliable** system, with the right **observability** and **operability**, will become easier to maintain and will enable experimentation to deliver the right value to their customers.
- **Easier Onboarding & Adoption**. When dev essential tasks are **automated**, there is less **documentation** to write and, subsequently, less to read to get started!
- **Improves employee attraction and retention**. A productive and effective developer is a **happy** developer.

Most importantly, this positive or negative impact will persist throughout the lifetime of the project. According to a [Forrester opportunity snapshot](https://humanitec.com/blog/key-findings-from-forrester-opportunity-snapshot?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block), as a result of improving DevEx:

- 74% of survey respondents said they can drive developer productivity
- 77% can shorten time to market
- 85% can impact revenue growth
- 75% can better attract and retain customers
- 82% can increase customer satisfaction

## What are key Developer Experience metrics?

### DORA Metrics

DORA framework (DevOps Research and Assessment) , which measures an organization’s DevOps performance, can be helpful. Key metrics include:

- **Deployment frequency** (DF): how frequently an organization releases new software
- **Lead time for changes** (LT): the time taken from when a change is requested or initiated to when it is deployed
- **Mean time to recovery** (MTTR): the average time it takes to recover from a failure
- **Change failure rate** (CFR): the percentage of changes that result in a failure

Other relevant metrics

- **Time To First Commit**
- **Time to First Deployment**
- **Time to Full System Setup**. Assuming a laptop/pc that has never run the solution, how long does it take to set up and run the whole system end-to-end and see a result.

## How to Improve Developer Experience?

Improve Onboarding

- Clear onboarding guides
- README files
- Assign mentor

Standardize Essential Tasks

- Local environment setup automation
- Build, test and deploy automation
- Coding standards and best practices

Observability

- System health monitoring
- Business metrics monitoring

Operability

- Rollback capabilities
- Enable Feature Flags

More frequent and smaller steps

- Refine User Stories (User Story Mapping)
- Small, more Frequent and Atomic Pull Requests

Reduce Cognitive Load

- Simply architecture
- Simply processes
- Minimize tools and dependencies
